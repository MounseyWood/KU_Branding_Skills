## SLIDE 3: What is Phygital?
**Visual:** Diagram showing spectrum of reality (physical → digital → virtual)

**Script:**
Phygital describes the seamless integration of physical and digital experiences. In design practice, it means working fluidly across realities:
- Translating physical objects, bodies and materials into digital twins
- Simulating processes and all possible outcomes before making
- Moving ourselves and designs between screens, workshops, production lines and immersive environments

This digital infrastructure enables 'thinking through making across the spectrum of reality' - designing and storytelling for in-life, on-life, and un-real-life contexts in real-time.

**Speaking Time:** 1.5 minutes

---

## SLIDE 4: Case Study - Clo3D in Fashion
**Visual:** Screenshots of Clo3D interface + student work examples

**Key Points:**
- Industry-standard 3D design and simulation software
- Used to improve design accuracy, reduce waste, reduce time to market
- Enhances creativity and fosters sustainable practices

**Script:**
Let me show you this in action. In the Fashion Department, students learn Clo3D—an industry-standard tool that's revolutionizing the fashion industry worldwide.

Students can:
- Create avatars of their muses or fit models
- Import and simulate traditionally created patterns
- Develop new patterns using flat pattern cutting and draping techniques they learn from day one
- Or explore digital-first methods: plotting shapes directly on the body, using AI to generate patterns from sketches

This isn't replacing traditional making—it's amplifying it. Students connect their cognitive design understanding through blended modalities.

**Speaking Time:** 2 minutes

## SLIDE 6: Democratizing Access - The Digital Practice Knowledge Base
**Visual:** Screenshot of Canvas site structure

**Script:**
To ensure equity of access, I've created a 'Digital Practice Knowledge Base' within our BA Fashion Canvas site. All students have open access to asynchronous teaching and learning materials.

These aren't just follow-along tutorials—they're contextual resources giving insight into current and emergent industrial practice.

Working with Hannah Jordan, we've mirrored all Level 4 Design Product resources in digital format. Students who engage with this in their personal study time arrive at Level 5 already ahead of the curve.

## SLIDE 7: The Challenge - Materials & Technology Gaps
**Visual:** Side-by-side comparison: what we have vs. what industry uses

**Current Limitations:**
- Basic visual capture only (photocopier, smartphone photography)
- No modern industry-standard technology for physical properties
- No advanced scanning for visual appearance

**Script (Honest Challenges):**
But here's where I want to be honest about our frustrations. For accurate physical outcomes, materials need both their visual properties AND physical performance captured.

Currently, we're using photocopiers and smartphones. We have no modern industry-standard technology for capturing physical properties or advanced visual appearance.

This is a significant gap between what our students can achieve and what industry expects.

**Speaking Time:** 1.5 minutes

## PRESENTATION TIPS

**Pacing:**
- Practice with a timer
- Mark where you're at 5 minutes, 10 minutes
- Have slides you can skip if running long (slides 6 or 9)

**Engagement:**
- Make eye contact
- Use conversational tone
- Show genuine enthusiasm for student work
- Be honest about frustrations (this is encouraged!)

**Technical:**
- Have backup PDF in case of connectivity issues
- Test any videos/animations beforehand
- Bring examples of student work if possible

**For Afternoon Discussion:**
- Take notes during others' presentations
- Look for collaboration opportunities
- Prepare 2-3 specific "next steps" suggestions
