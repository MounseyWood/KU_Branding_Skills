## POTENTIAL QUESTIONS & SUGGESTED RESPONSES

Based on your presentation, here are likely questions and how you might respond:

### Q: "What's the cost of implementing Clo3D across the faculty?"

**Response:** "Great question. Clo3D offers educational licenses that are significantly more affordable than commercial licenses. The bigger consideration is communal compute infrastructure—shared servers and processing power—which would actually reduce per-department costs compared to individual solutions. The Fashion Department started with a modest pilot and we'd be happy to share our cost-benefit analysis with any interested departments."

---

### Q: "How do you assess digital work vs physical making?"

**Response:** "We've adapted our rubrics to value both equally. Students are assessed on their design thinking, technical execution, and professional presentation—whether that's a physical garment, a digital render, or both. The key is that digital enhances rather than replaces physical making. Students still need to understand materials, construction, and craft. The digital tools amplify their ability to explore, iterate, and communicate."

---

### Q: "What if staff don't have the digital skills to teach this?"

**Response:** "That's exactly why professional development time is so critical. I didn't have Clo3D skills three years ago—I learned alongside developing the curriculum. We need protected time for staff to develop these skills, access to training resources, and a culture where it's okay to be a learner. The Digital Practice Knowledge Base approach works for staff too—asynchronous, self-paced, contextual learning."

---

### Q: "Won't this make education less personal, more automated?"

**Response:** "Actually, the opposite. By using digital tools for iteration and exploration, we free up studio time for the high-touch, personal mentoring that students value most. Instead of spending three hours cutting a toile that doesn't work, students can test digitally first, then spend that studio time on refinement and creative development with staff feedback. Digital enables more experimentation, which leads to richer conversations."

---

### Q: "How does this align with sustainability goals?"

**Response:** "Directly. Digital iteration means less material waste. Students can test hundreds of designs before cutting into fabric. The Phygital Materials Bureau specifically supports UN SDG 12—Responsible Consumption and Production. We're teaching students to design sustainably from the start, to understand material properties before making, and to create digital assets that can have multiple applications beyond single physical products."

---

### Q: "What about students who struggle with technology?"

**Response:** "This is why equity of access is central to our approach. The Digital Practice Knowledge Base ensures all students have resources regardless of prior experience. We teach digital skills progressively from Level 4, starting with foundations. And crucially, peer support emerges—students become digital advocates and leaders within their cohort. But you're right to highlight this challenge. Without adequate equipment and support, we risk a two-tier system, which is unacceptable."
